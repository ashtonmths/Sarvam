## 1. Parties and roles

**Controller:** the customer organisation using Sadhak.
**Processor:** Sadhak (the Finite Loop Club project).

The Controller decides which systems to connect and which channels to mine. The
Processor processes personal data only to provide the service, and only on the
Controller's documented instructions — of which connecting a connector and
selecting a mining scope are the operative ones.

## 2. Subject matter and duration

The Processor maintains a dependency graph of the Controller's connected
systems, together with the reasoning behind those dependencies, and evaluates
proposed changes against it. Processing lasts as long as the Controller's
organisation exists, and ends when the Controller deletes it.

## 3. Nature of the data

This section is unusually short, and the reason is architectural rather than
editorial.

**Processed:**

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| Structural metadata | Table, column, workflow, report and API names, their types, and the references between them |
| Discussion excerpts | Quoted spans of text from channels the Controller selects for mining, with author name and permalink |
| Account data | Name, email, hashed password, organisation membership |
| Operational records | Verdicts, decisions, incidents and an audit log of privileged actions |

**Not processed:** the contents of the Controller's records. Sadhak indexes that
a field named `invoices.vat_rate` exists and which report reads it. It does not
read an invoice. This is enforced at the connector boundary, where crawlers emit
schema and wiring only, and it is verified by an automated test that plants
canary strings in cell values and fails the build if any of them reaches
Sadhak's database.

The only personal data of any sensitivity is therefore the **discussion
excerpts** — a colleague's message explaining why something exists, with their
name attached — and the account data of the Controller's own users.

## 4. Security measures

- Credentials are sealed with authenticated encryption before storage. The
  additional authenticated data binds each credential to its organisation,
  connector instance, scope and kind, so ciphertext moved to another
  organisation's row fails to decrypt rather than opening.
- Read and write credentials are separate records. Crawling uses read scope and
  cannot be handed a write credential by accident.
- Tenancy is enforced at the credential, not from request input. An organisation
  id in a path is asserted against the one the credential resolved to, and a
  mismatch answers 404 rather than 403 — a wrong organisation id is
  indistinguishable from one that does not exist.
- Outbound requests pass an egress guard with DNS pinning and per-connector host
  allowlists.
- All privileged actions are recorded in an append-only audit log.
- Transport is TLS throughout; secrets are redacted from logs by configured
  paths rather than by best effort at each call site.

## 5. Subprocessors

The current list, with purpose and data touched, is published at
<https://sadhak.online/legal/subprocessors> and is the authoritative version.

At the time of writing: **Hostinger** (VPS hosting, India — all data at rest),
**Cloudflare** (DNS, TLS and proxy — all traffic in transit), **OpenRouter**
(LLM inference — graph structure and mined discussion excerpts), and **GitHub**
(source and container images — no customer data).

Notably absent: any embedding vendor. Semantic search over rationale runs a
model on the Processor's own server, so that text is not shipped to a third
party for that purpose.

The Processor gives the Controller at least 30 days' notice before adding a
subprocessor. The Controller may object, and the remedy is to disconnect the
affected connector or terminate without penalty.

## 6. International transfers

Data at rest is held in India. Traffic passes through Cloudflare's global
network, and inference requests reach the United States and onward to the
provider serving the model. Where a transfer requires them, the parties rely on
the appropriate Standard Contractual Clauses, incorporated by reference and to
be attached on execution.

## 7. Assistance to the Controller

- **Data subject requests.** The Processor provides a self-serve export of
  everything belonging to an organisation, and self-serve deletion, both from
  organisation settings. For anything those do not cover, the Processor responds
  within 30 days.
- **Breach notification.** The Processor notifies the Controller without undue
  delay and in any case within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data
  breach, with what is known at the time rather than waiting for a complete
  picture.
- **Audits.** The source is public. The Processor will additionally answer
  reasonable written questions and supply evidence of the controls above.

## 8. Deletion and return

On termination, the Controller may export everything as a single JSON document
before deleting. Deleting an organisation removes its graph, rationale,
verdicts, decisions, credentials and audit log by database-level cascade.

There is no grace period and no soft delete. That is deliberate: a thirty-day
window during which the Processor still holds everything is a retention policy
wearing a deletion policy's name. The Controller is asked to type the
organisation name to confirm, which is what stands in for a recovery window.

Backups are the one honest exception — a deleted organisation may persist in an
encrypted backup until it rotates out. Backups are not readable by the service
and are not used to restore individual organisations.

## 9. Liability

Liability under this agreement follows the [Terms of
Service](https://sadhak.online/legal/terms). During open beta the service is
free, and the limitation of liability reflects that.
